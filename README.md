# US Consumer Passenger EV Market Intelligence Agent

An AI-assisted market intelligence project built with Amazon QuickSuite to examine competition, affordability, charging infrastructure, and the US consumer passenger electric vehicle (EV) market outlook toward 2030.

## Project Overview

This project used Amazon QuickSuite to investigate the US consumer passenger EV market by combining structured vehicle specification data with external market research. The objective was to identify market trends, competitive shifts, affordability challenges, infrastructure gaps, and future uncertainties to support strategic decision-making.

The research focused on the 2025 market benchmark, relevant 2026 developments, and the outlook toward 2030.

## Tools and Data

- **Amazon QuickSuite Space:** Organized the project workspace and materials.
- **Quick Research:** Gathered external market intelligence.
- **Market Intelligence Agent:** Supported analysis and synthesis of the dataset and external research.
- **Internal dataset:** `ElectricCarData_Clean.csv`, sourced from Kaggle, containing specifications for 98 electric vehicle models.

## Project Workflow

1. Uploaded the EV dataset and organized the project materials in Amazon QuickSuite Space.
2. Used Quick Research to investigate manufacturer market share, vehicle pricing, charging infrastructure, policy developments, battery costs, and 2030 forecasts.
3. Used the Market Intelligence Agent to synthesize the dataset and external research into a Market Analysis.
4. Evaluated the findings for evidence quality, consistency, timeliness, confidence, and limitations.
5. Prepared a Market Intelligence Brief communicating the findings and their implications for leadership.

## Key Findings

- **Competition:** Tesla showed a strong average-range position in the internal dataset, while external research reported declining US battery electric vehicle (BEV) market share during 2025 and gains by other manufacturers.
- **Affordability:** Lower-priced models represented in the dataset had shorter average ranges. External research also identified a price premium for EVs over the overall new-vehicle market.
- **Vehicle body styles:** SUVs accounted for 43 of the 98 models in the dataset, making them the largest body-style category within the dataset.
- **2030 outlook:** External forecasts differed substantially, highlighting the need for scenario-based planning rather than reliance on a single projection.
- **Charging infrastructure:** External research identified a gap between estimated public charging availability and projected infrastructure requirements.

The findings distinguish vehicle specifications and dataset composition from external evidence about US market performance, infrastructure, policy, and forecasts.

## Limitations and Confidence

The internal dataset contains European-priced vehicle specifications rather than US sales data. It does not provide time-series market data or cover charging infrastructure, policy developments, demographics, or 2030 forecasts. GM and Rivian are also absent from the dataset.

US market-share figures, transaction prices, infrastructure estimates, policy developments, and future projections therefore rely on external research. The overall confidence assessment was **Medium**, reflecting useful evidence on market dynamics alongside gaps in dataset coverage and uncertainty in future projections.

The findings should not be interpreted as a direct forecast of US EV sales or as proof that a single market development will determine future adoption.

## Project Outputs

- **Amazon QuickSuite Space:** Contains the organized project materials.

## Conclusion

The US Consumer Passenger EV Market Intelligence Agent demonstrates an AI-assisted market intelligence workflow using Amazon QuickSuite. It combines structured data with external research, synthesizes market signals, evaluates evidence limitations, and communicates findings to support strategic decision-making.
